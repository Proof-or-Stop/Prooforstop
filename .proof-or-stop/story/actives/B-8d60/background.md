There are two independent breaks. First, all 32 receipts carry artifactPath and ledgerPath literals under '.lattice/' inside the HMAC-signed payload, and the verifier resolves receipts[0].parentHead (d87f13e8) at the new '.proof-or-stop/' path, which does not exist at that commit; the genesis anchor therefore fails before any HMAC is checked. Second, the HMAC key is stored in the git common directory and is therefore clone-local; the repository was re-cloned on 2026-08-30 (reflog: clone from GitHub) and neither proof-or-stop-gap-log-provenance.key nor a lattice-named predecessor exists now, so the 32 legacy HMACs cannot be re-verified by anyone. The current findings.json digest equals receipt 32's postArtifactDigest, so no writer bypassed the ledger. A broken chain also makes done_prepare keep B-META dirt blocking and makes the scope classifier treat B-META edits as out of scope.

## Solution Tradeoffs
Option A, re-sign the legacy receipts with a new key after rewriting their path literals: restores a single contiguous chain but fabricates authenticity for records nobody can verify, and rewrites bytes the identity-cutover decision freezes as provenance; rejected. Option B, delete the ledger and start from genesis: trivially unblocks gap_log but discards 32 receipts of audit trail and teaches that a re-clone erases history; rejected as the primary path. Option C, rename-aware verification plus an explicit audited re-anchor: the legacy segment stays byte-frozen and is verified structurally (sequence, links, digest continuity, genesis anchor at the historical path) but marked key-lost; a signed epoch-boundary receipt under a fresh key binds the legacy bytes digest and the current committed artifact; new receipts extend the new epoch. This is chosen because it is honest about what can and cannot be verified, matches the init_check_repair pattern of an acknowledged, reasoned, auditable fallback, and survives the next rename or re-clone. Option C costs a new command, a schema-aware verifier, and consumer updates.
