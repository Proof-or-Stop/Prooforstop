## Key Insights
run-batch.mjs (M2) is append-only + identity-keyed (iterationId=replay/<scale>/<index>) which is exactly what a resume needs, but it does NOT yet support resume: it runs the full campaign from scratch and its strict exact-multiset end-of-run guard would reject a restart into a partial ledger. M3 adds an ADDITIVE resume mode: on --resume, read the existing ledger, skip iterationIds already recorded (proof OR stop), run only the remaining, then reconcile the FINAL multiset. Because the record append is one atomic JSONL line, a kill BEFORE the append leaves no record (a clean re-run on resume); a kill DURING the append could leave a truncated trailing line (resume must ignore/repair a malformed last line). H3 = 100% of iterations terminate in exactly one ledger record after the probed kills + resume; the ledger identity multiset equals the protocol cross-product exactly (0 dup / 0 gap); the mid-iteration casualty resolves to a stop record or a clean re-run, never a silent half-record. The mid-iteration crash hook already exists in run-batch as POS_FAIL_APPEND_AT (fault-injection: fail at the append step = after workspace write, before ledger append). resume-probe.mjs is a committed producer; a wall-clock (uncontrolled) kill may be a secondary trial but the deterministic hook is the scored one.

## Solution Tradeoffs
resume-probe.mjs orchestrates run-batch (small scale for speed): boundary kill (SIGKILL after K recorded) + mid-iteration crash (POS_FAIL_APPEND_AT), each followed by a --resume restart, then multiset reconciliation into resume-reconciliation.json. Adding resume to run-batch (rather than a separate resumable driver) keeps ONE driver that is both the M2 campaign producer and the M3 resume target — the evidence stays coherent. The resume mode is additive (empty-ledger fresh path unchanged) so M2 batch-ledger.jsonl + selfcheck-m2 stay valid. Rejected alternative: a separate resume-only driver — would duplicate the driver and let the two drift.
