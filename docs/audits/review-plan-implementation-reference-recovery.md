## Cause and implementation

`reviewTriageAuthorityViolation` delegates authority-bearing text to a fail-closed finite grammar. The accepted reference forms did not include implementation of a numbered section. Generic sentence splitting also treats decimal section punctuation as sentence boundaries.

`maskBoundedPlanImplementationReferences` recognizes only a complete normalized sentence of the following form before generic authority segmentation. Before that projection, the classifier compares every normalized dependency-bearing match with an exact raw `B-<four hexadecimal characters>` match. Separator loss during normalization therefore cannot turn `B--xxxx`, underscore, slash, or missing-hyphen spellings into an admitted reference.

```text
Implement Section <positive integer[.numeric component...]> of the
accepted|approved Dynamic Finding [System] [execution] plan
[after B-<four hexadecimal characters> is accepted and merged].
```

The line wrapping above is explanatory, not another accepted syntax. Recognition is temporary classifier input projection; stored descriptions and assembled prompt text retain their original bytes. No Story ID or full-description allowlist was introduced. Other sentences and fields still pass through the existing checks. Controlled-triage classification continues to consume the original source and original segments.

A bounded reference also retains a finite dependent-continuation check before projection. Every remaining sentence or semicolon clause in the same source is scanned with the plan reference itself as the live antecedent. Direct `its`, `this`, or `that` authority references, bare `it`/`them`/`these`/`those` shorthand, and the finite nominal forms `the same`, `the former`, and `the latter` are fail-closed immediately and across later clauses. Bare `this`/`that` is also bound in two finite positions: an object at the end of a clause with up to five allowlisted modifiers, or a clause-initial subject in a structurally complete mutation or stable-state relation. One shared finite modifier vocabulary applies before relation terms, between modal, polarity, passive auxiliary and predicate slots, and after a complete target or predicate where that position is grammatical for the bounded relation. Safe forms retain the five-modifier bound; detection consumes additional adjacent allowlisted modifiers, including both sides of obligation polarity and `to`, only to fail closed instead of erasing the live target. Once a bare-subject passive mutation, direct copula mutation (`is|are|was|were` plus a recognized mutation stem), or stable-state prefix is structurally complete, remaining same-clause text keeps that authority live and fails closed unless the whole relation is one of the finite safe forms. A bare active mutation without a recognized object fails closed when its normalized residual has an optional finite connector and modifiers followed by an explicit mutation or passive-review predicate; this catches `This may update and reviewed.` and `This may update and then reviewed.` without misclassifying compound determiner controls such as `This May update and next release preserve...`. A negative stable-state relation over `unchanged`, `preserved`, `retained`, or `unmodified` is independently unsafe both in direct-predicate forms and in `do|modal + not|never + remain` compositions. The subject relation recognizes the finite obligation complements `is|are|was|were [required] to`, with modifier and explicit-polarity slots between the copula, optional `required`, `to`, passive auxiliary and predicate. The same bounded obligation-negative composition is used for an active mutation over an explicit coreferent object and for a passive mutation; only an explicit `not|never` makes either form safe. Bare `this|that` may also act in an explicitly negative active relation over a recognized coreferent target. An active mutation must end with only allowlisted modifiers or bind a recognized coreferent object; a mutation-like noun followed by ordinary prose is not enough. This admits bounded determiner phrases including compound forms that begin `This May update` or `This Will change`, while still rejecting lowercase active authority mutations, passive modifier interleavings, affirmative passive mutations with trailing same-clause text, modifier overflow, and obligation mutations. This also keeps prose such as `This high-risk feature` outside the shorthand relation. Only complete, bounded safe forms are admitted: an allowlisted inspection action, an explicit active or passive negation of a recognized mutation, or an affirmative unchanged/preserved state. All four safe forms consume the same finite target and modifier relations. Negating preservation or retention is not safe. This avoids claiming arbitrary English classification while keeping the enforced reference projection finite.

The optional dependency clause is a syntactic reference, not a receipt that its target is accepted or merged. Existing dependency and lifecycle gates retain that responsibility. This finite change makes no claim to classify arbitrary natural language safely.
