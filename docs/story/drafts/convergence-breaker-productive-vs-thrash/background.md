## Trigger Event

On 2026-09-04 another session on this machine reported that all three of its stories, among them B-c86f, were frozen by the convergence breaker at the advance signal with their single auto-recovery already spent, and that the advances which spent the budget were mostly productive: a second-vendor review lane had found eleven genuine defects across the three, and the fix for the last of them is what tripped the final freeze. The brake did stop an unbounded loop and is not being argued to be wrong. What is being reported is that a story which keeps surfacing real defects is indistinguishable, from the counter's point of view, from one thrashing on the same failure.

## Key Insights

Two claims here were verified against packages/main/src/core/convergence-breaker.ts at the time of writing; the operational observations are attributed to the reporting session and are not independently confirmed.

The breaker measures every signal as a delta over a persisted baseline, and the advance signal trips when advanceCount minus advanceBaseline exceeds maxAdvance (line 261, strict greater-than). On a trip with recovery budget remaining, the graduated action re-baselines all signals to the current counts and spends the one recovery, and the advance proceeds; when the budget is exhausted the next trip freezes.

That produces a specific readability defect, visible in the code rather than inferred. The reason string is built from the DELTA, not the raw counter: `advance-count ${advanceDelta}>${thresholds.maxAdvance}`. So a story at raw count 21 against baseline 0 and a story at raw count 42 against baseline 21 both record the identical string, `advance-count 21>20`. The persisted state therefore reads as though nothing has changed between the recovery and the freeze, and a reader who subtracts the baseline from the count to predict remaining headroom gets an answer that the retained trip reason appears to contradict. The reporting session made that prediction twice and was wrong both times, once in writing to its user.

The proposal that a productive round can be distinguished from a thrashing one by whether its advances closed newly filed verified findings is right in shape but incomplete, and the reporting session named the case it misclassifies. Their advances fell into three kinds, not two: rounds that closed newly filed verified findings; re-attempts of a gate that refused for the same reason, which for them was a load-sensitive smoke that fails only while another session holds the validation slot; and forced re-work with no finding attached, which was main moving underneath them across four separate re-merges, each costing an evidence refresh and a fresh reviewer receipt, plus one cycle spent because done_required_validate was run under raised timeout environment variables, which are not stripped from the effective environment digest and so cause the gate to reject an otherwise passing suite. A signal keyed only on closed findings scores that third kind as thrash. It is waste, but not the runaway kind the brake exists to stop, and a story arguably should not be frozen for the repository moving underneath it.

## Solution Tradeoffs

The cheapest improvement is independent of the classification question: record the raw counters alongside the delta in the trip reason, so two different trips do not produce the same string, and so the persisted state cannot be read as contradicting itself. That is a small change to the reason construction and to what the state retains.

The classification itself is the harder half and should be decided rather than assumed. Keying on closed findings alone is known to misclassify forced re-work; adding a third outcome for advances attributable to external drift, such as a merge that moved main or an evidence refresh that changed no story-owned file, would cover the reported case, at the cost of a signal that a story could in principle be steered into. Whether such advances should be excluded from the budget entirely, or merely reported differently, is the decision. Doing nothing is also defensible: the brake is a hard backstop and a human ack is a low bar, and the reporting session did not argue the freezes were wrong.
